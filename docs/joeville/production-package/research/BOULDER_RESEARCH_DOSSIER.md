# Boulder Research Dossier for Joeville

**Research date:** 2026-07-13  
**Role:** Boulder Research Director / Location and Clearance Research  
**Scope:** Geography, orientation, signature locations, climate, vegetation, architectural history, cultural signals, current-business checks, and production-clearance risks.  
**Use:** Source-grounded input for the Joeville world map, visual-development bible, location roster, and legal/clearance review. This is not legal advice.

## Executive production findings

1. **Build Joeville against a western mountain wall.** Boulder Valley is bounded by mountains on the west. From the town, the recurring mountain-facing view is therefore broadly westward; the plains/open valley extend eastward. This is the most important geographic continuity rule.
2. **Treat the creek as the town's cross-axis.** Boulder's official creek path runs from Boulder Canyon at the west end, through downtown, to eastern Boulder. Joeville's creek corridor can credibly connect the canyon gateway, civic/market district, downtown, and eastern neighborhoods.
3. **Keep the falls outside town.** Boulder Falls is 11 miles west of Boulder on Boulder Canyon Drive. It is a distinct canyon excursion involving a road approach and highway crossing, not a waterfall behind downtown. Joeville's falls should require a visible travel transition.
4. **Use a layered urban texture, not a mountain-resort monoculture.** Boulder combines late-nineteenth-century commercial blocks, a four-block brick pedestrian mall, protected historic districts, Chautauqua's cottages and assembly buildings, postwar neighborhoods, and contemporary infill.
5. **Seasonal continuity must allow snow and green overlap.** NOAA's 1991–2020 Boulder normals show substantial March and April snowfall while April and May are also the wettest normal months. Spring plates should include wet brick, leaf-out, flowers, and plausible late snow variants.
6. **Wellness, coffee, local food, outdoor movement, and community gathering are supported cultural signals—but should not become caricatures.** The official destination bureau foregrounds yoga, meditation, health, independent coffee, farmers markets, outdoor exercise, and food events. Use these as activity patterns across the ensemble, not as a single visual joke.
7. **Default to fictional businesses and original scenic design.** The actual Whole Foods Market Pearl store is currently listed at 2905 Pearl Street. Its name, logos, branded departments, and source-identifying presentation should not appear in Joeville without clearance. A wholly original grocery analogue is the lowest-friction recurring set.

## 1. Verified factual baseline

Everything in this section is a researched fact or a narrowly stated observation from a cited source. Production recommendations and creative extrapolations appear later under **Design inferences**.

### 1.1 Town orientation and landform

- The City of Boulder describes the Boulder Valley planning area's western boundary as **the mountains on the west**. The same planning framework distinguishes the urbanized city from rural-preservation land around it. ([City of Boulder: Boulder Valley Comprehensive Plan](https://bouldercolorado.gov/projects/boulder-valley-comprehensive-plan))
- The Flatirons are tilted rock slabs associated with Green Mountain and are a defining OSMP symbol. City geology materials describe the foothill geology and the mesa surfaces at the mountain front. ([City of Boulder: Geology on OSMP](https://bouldercolorado.gov/geology-osmp))
- Chautauqua lies on a southwestern mesa overlooking Boulder Valley. The historic district is bordered by open space to its south and west and has operated as a cultural and recreational enclave since 1898. ([City of Boulder: Chautauqua Historic District overview](https://bouldercolorado.gov/sites/default/files/2020-12/chautauquaoverview.pdf))
- The Chautauqua Meadow Trail is a short, visible approach across open meadow; the city's trail record describes it as 0.5 mile with 172 feet of elevation gain. ([City of Boulder: Meadow Trail](https://bouldercolorado.gov/trail/meadow))

**Continuity consequence from the facts:** Downtown-to-mountain sightlines should place the dominant ridge/Flatirons mass broadly west. Chautauqua belongs uphill and southwest of the core, not east on the plains.

### 1.2 Boulder Creek and the west-east connective corridor

- The Boulder Creek Path is a 5.5-mile multi-use path extending from Boulder Canyon at the west end to Stazio Ballfields just past 55th Street at the east end. It passes through downtown and by the Main Library, Civic Area Park, and the Boulder Dushanbe Teahouse. ([City of Boulder: Boulder Creek Path](https://bouldercolorado.gov/locations/boulder-creek-path))
- Cottonwoods line Boulder Creek in eastern Boulder County, and the streamside corridor supports dense foliage and wildlife movement. ([City of Boulder: Habitat Conservation Areas](https://bouldercolorado.gov/habitat-conservation-areas))
- The city maintains instream-flow agreements and monitors protected reaches, confirming that creek flow is managed and seasonally variable rather than a fixed decorative water feature. ([City of Boulder: Instream Flow Program](https://bouldercolorado.gov/services/instream-flow-program))

**Continuity consequence from the facts:** The creek can credibly shift from rockier, confined western/canyon character to urban civic banks and then broader cottonwood-lined eastern reaches.

### 1.3 Chautauqua character and function

- Boulder residents funded the Chautauqua project in the late 1890s; the site opened in 1898. The place historically combined educational, political, recreational, and cultural programming. ([City of Boulder: Chautauqua Park](https://bouldercolorado.gov/locations/chautauqua-park))
- The Colorado Chautauqua is a National Historic Landmark. The city-owned ensemble includes the 1898 Auditorium, 1898 Dining Hall, and 1900 Academic Hall, together with a cottage landscape and open-space setting. ([City of Boulder: Chautauqua Collaborative Stewardship Program](https://bouldercolorado.gov/services/chautauqua-collaborative-stewardship-program))
- The official trailhead page describes high use, limited/challenging parking during good weather, trail access, lodging, dining, concerts, cultural events, education, and recreation. ([City of Boulder: Chautauqua Trailhead](https://bouldercolorado.gov/trailhead/chautauqua))

**Continuity consequence from the facts:** Chautauqua is both a threshold to the mountains and a programmed community campus. It can inspire several Joeville sets—meadow, historic hall, dining porch, cottage lane, and trail gateway—without collapsing them into one background.

### 1.4 Boulder Falls

- Boulder Falls is 11 miles west of Boulder on the north side of Boulder Canyon Drive/SR 119 between Boulder and Nederland. The official page describes a short walk, rocks, a compact waterfall, parking across the highway, and no formal trailhead. ([City of Boulder: Boulder Falls](https://bouldercolorado.gov/trail/boulder-falls))
- The official trail listing showed **Closed** during this research pass. Status is operational and can change; it must be rechecked before any real-world scout or shoot. The page also gives the trail as 0.10 mile with 63 feet of gain. ([City of Boulder: Boulder Falls](https://bouldercolorado.gov/trail/boulder-falls))
- The location was donated to the city in 1914 and is connected to the area's mining-era history. ([City of Boulder: Boulder Falls](https://bouldercolorado.gov/trail/boulder-falls))

**Continuity consequence from the facts:** A Joeville falls sequence needs a canyon-road transition, an arrival/parking or drop-off beat, a road-crossing implication, and a short foot approach. It should not be visible from the downtown grid.

### 1.5 Pearl Street and downtown spatial language

- The Pearl Street Mall is a **four-block** pedestrian corridor between 11th and 15th Streets. The city designated it the Downtown Boulder Mall on August 6, 1977, prohibiting cars on those blocks. ([City of Boulder: Pearl Street Mall](https://bouldercolorado.gov/locations/pearl-street-mall))
- The city's cultural-landscape assessment describes a linear space extending facade-to-facade, unified by brick paving, mature shade trees, planters, and recurring gathering/play/art nodes. Each block varies while retaining a shared material vocabulary. ([City of Boulder: Pearl Street Pedestrian Mall Cultural Landscape Assessment](https://bouldercolorado.gov/pa/media/11760/download))
- The mall lies within the Downtown Historic District. The district developed from the original 1859 townsite, mining-support commerce, and the arrival of the state university; many early commercial buildings remain. ([City of Boulder: Downtown Historic District overview](https://bouldercolorado.gov/sites/default/files/2020-12/downtownoverview.pdf))
- Boulder has an active preservation system with more than 1,300 designated properties, including more than 200 individual landmarks and 10 historic districts. The Downtown Boulder Historic District was listed on the National Register in 1978. ([City of Boulder: Landmarks and Historic Districts](https://bouldercolorado.gov/services/landmarks-and-historic-districts))

**Continuity consequence from the facts:** The recognizable downtown grammar is low-to-mid-rise street walls, narrow storefront rhythms, brick and durable masonry, mature canopy, human-scale street furniture, and block-specific activity pockets—not one monumental plaza.

| Annual mean daily maximum / minimum | 65.7°F / 37.3°F |
| January mean daily maximum / minimum | 47.0°F / 21.5°F |
| July mean daily maximum / minimum | 88.0°F / 57.0°F |

### 1.7 Vegetation and habitat palette

- City habitat materials distinguish dry meadows, ponderosa pine, drought-tolerant shrubs, cool canyons, and dense thickets. ([City of Boulder: Habitat Conservation Areas](https://bouldercolorado.gov/habitat-conservation-areas))
- City native-plant guidance places narrowleaf cottonwood along stream banks and valleys, plains cottonwood in moist floodplains/riparian areas, and ponderosa pine in dry sunny sites. It also lists blue grama and little bluestem among native grasses. ([City of Boulder: Native Plants Growing Tips](https://bouldercolorado.gov/native-plants-growing-tips))
- Boulder's urban forest is not exclusively native. The city's urban-forest plan records a mix including green ash, Siberian elm, honeylocust, crabapple, maple, spruce, pine, cottonwood, and others. ([City of Boulder: Urban Forest Strategic Plan](https://bouldercolorado.gov/sites/default/files/2021-05/boulderufspv2018.pdf))
- The city's approved tree and plant list, effective March 7, 2026, explicitly emphasizes water conservation and wildfire resilience. ([City of Boulder: Tree and Plant List](https://bouldercolorado.gov/tree-and-plant-list))

**Continuity consequence from the facts:** Use distinct vegetation zones: cottonwood/willow and lush understory at water; mature mixed deciduous canopy downtown and in older neighborhoods; open grasses and shrubs on mesas; ponderosa and rock at foothill/canyon sets. Avoid applying alpine spruce forest to the entire town.

### 1.8 Architecture and historical layering

- Boulder's downtown grew from an 1859 townsite serving mining settlements and later the university. Substantial commercial blocks and narrow storefronts established the late-nineteenth-century core. ([City of Boulder: Downtown Historic District overview](https://bouldercolorado.gov/sites/default/files/2020-12/downtownoverview.pdf))
- The city's preservation review criteria explicitly consider style, arrangement, texture, color, and materials; city project-review materials identify brick, stone, wood, architectural metal, and related durable materials as high-quality materials. ([City of Boulder: Landmark Alteration Certificate Review](https://bouldercolorado.gov/services/landmark-alteration-certificate-application-and-review); [City of Boulder: Land Use Site Review Guide](https://bouldercolorado.gov/land-use-site-review-application-guide))
- The city's postwar survey frames Boulder's development through mining, agriculture, education, lifestyle, and industrial expansion, documenting a significant post-World War II residential layer rather than a purely Victorian town. ([City of Boulder: Post-World War II Residential Architecture Survey](https://bouldercolorado.gov/sites/default/files/2021-02/ww2-survey-final.pdf))
- Chautauqua contributes a separate rustic civic/cottage idiom, while the Civic Area also contains Art Deco and modernist elements. ([City of Boulder: Self-Guided Public Art Tour](https://bouldercolorado.gov/boulder-self-guided-public-art-tour))

### 1.9 Wellness, food, coffee, and gathering culture

The following sources are official destination-marketing sources, so they are useful evidence of publicly promoted identity and current activity, not neutral ethnography.

- Visit Boulder ties local wellness identity to a history of health retreats and currently promotes yoga, meditation, wellness events, outdoor exercise, healthy restaurants, and tea culture. ([Visit Boulder: Health & Wellness](https://www.bouldercoloradousa.com/things-to-do/health-and-wellness/))
- The Boulder Farmers Market operates outdoors at 13th Street beside Boulder Creek and civic/cultural venues; the listed 2026 seasonal schedule is Saturdays April–November and Wednesdays May–October. The market is presented as a local-food and community hub with food, seating, shade, music, and gathering. ([Visit Boulder: Farmers Market guide](https://www.bouldercoloradousa.com/things-to-do/insider-guides/farmers-market/))
- Visit Boulder's current coffee guide lists more than four dozen independent coffee shops, supporting coffee meetings as a recurring local activity rather than a single branded-location gag. ([Visit Boulder: Coffee Crawl](https://www.bouldercoloradousa.com/food-and-drink/coffee-shops/))

## 2. Design inferences for Joeville

This section is **creative interpretation**, not a claim about actual Boulder geography or culture.

### 2.1 Recommended geographic translation

- Establish Joeville with a stable compass: mountains west; open valley/eastern neighborhoods east; downtown near the creek; meadow/cultural campus uphill southwest; canyon road departing west; falls well beyond the urban edge.
- Make **Joe Creek** the legible connective tissue. Its western reach is stone-lined and fast; the civic reach has paths, bridges, lawn, market access, and public art; the eastern reach broadens under cottonwoods.
- Create a dedicated **Canyon Gate** transition set where the urban street grid narrows into rock walls and the road begins to climb. This allows episodes to communicate travel to the falls without a long drive sequence.
- Treat **Castle Grace** as an original Joeville landmark and choose its placement only after the showrunner resolves its story function. The most coherent candidates are: (A) a western bench above town, visually powerful but separated from downtown; or (B) a wooded south-bank rise near the creek, more accessible but less mountain-dominant. Do not place it where it breaks the creek/falls relationship.

### 2.2 Recommended recurring-location analogues

| Boulder research anchor | Joeville function | Translation rule |
|---|---|---|
| Pearl Street Mall | Main pedestrian social spine | Original four-block-equivalent walk with brick/stone ground plane, trees, varied gathering nodes, and fictional storefronts |
| Boulder Creek Path | Walking-talk and transition corridor | Continuous path family with west/civic/east variants and repeatable bridges |
| Chautauqua | Meadow, trailhead, culture, dining | Split into Meadow Commons, Assembly Hall, Dining Porch, Cottage Lane, and Trail Gate sets |
| Boulder Falls | Wonder, reflection, comic excursion | Remote canyon set with travel transition; original rock silhouette and waterfall composition |
| Farmers Market | Ensemble gathering and seasonal food | Recurring modular market dressing on the civic creek edge; spring/summer/autumn produce swaps |
| Boulder coffee culture | Intimate and small-group dialogue | Two original recurring cafés with distinct emotional functions rather than copies of real businesses |
| Wellness culture | Meditation, dance, conflict, community | Original flexible studio/hall plus outdoor meadow practice; avoid generic mysticism as the sole local identity |
| Whole Foods Market Pearl | Grocery encounters and meal setup | Fully fictional **independent** grocery concept with original name, facade, palette, departments, fixtures, and signage |

### 2.3 Seasonal visual system inferred from the climate record

- **Winter:** bare deciduous branches, patchy or deep snow variants, bright low sun, cold blue shade, plowed/wet pedestrian surfaces, evergreen structure visible.
- **Early spring:** dormant grasses with new green, muddy trail closures, wet rock, swelling creek, leafless-to-budding canopy, optional heavy wet snow.
- **Late spring:** saturated meadow greens, flowers, running water, variable clouds, lingering snow-event alternate plates.
- **Summer:** full canopy, sun-bleached grasses away from irrigation and water, intense high sun, cool cottonwood shade, afternoon-cloud/storm alternate.
- **Autumn:** yellow cottonwoods along water, mixed street-tree color, copper/red native grasses, crisp air, early-snow alternate.

Create weather variants as layer groups, not unrelated redraws: sky; distant mountain; midground vegetation; ground moisture/snow; water state; practical lights; atmospheric overlay.

## 3. Location, branding, and clearance findings

### 3.1 Real-world filming and scouting

- City filming rules cover city facilities, streets, sidewalks, Pearl Street Mall, parks, and open space. Applications must be submitted at least 14 days before a proposed shoot. Beginning in summer 2026, the city lists daily fees for medium and large productions; insurance is required, and people filmed must be released. ([City of Boulder: Film or Photography Permit](https://bouldercolorado.gov/services/film-permit-application))
- Most filming on City of Boulder Open Space and Mountain Parks land also requires an OSMP commercial-use permit. Permits do not grant exclusive use. Drone launch, landing, or operation from/on OSMP land is prohibited. ([City of Boulder: OSMP Permits](https://bouldercolorado.gov/osmp-permits); [City of Boulder: OSMP Commercial Use Permits](https://bouldercolorado.gov/services/osmp-commercial-use-permits))
- **Boulder County open space is a different jurisdiction.** Its current special-use page says permits will not be issued for commercial activities such as filming movies or commercials. Do not treat a City of Boulder permit as county authorization. ([Boulder County: Special Use Permits on Open Space](https://bouldercounty.gov/open-space/activities/permits/))
- A public-space permit does not supply access to or permission for privately owned interiors, roofs, patios, storefront staging areas, or private parking. Obtain a location agreement/property release from the owner or authorized representative.
- Source-site photographs are research references, not automatically licensed production assets. Commission original scout photography or obtain explicit licenses before using photographs as textures, plates, or close derivative elements.

### 3.2 Architecture, art, and interiors

- Federal law permits pictorial representations of constructed architectural works located in or ordinarily visible from a public place. ([17 U.S.C. §120(a), U.S. House Office of the Law Revision Counsel](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title17-section120))
- That rule is not a universal clearance. It does not authorize trespass, private interior access, reproduction of copyrighted murals/sculpture, use of copyrighted source photographs, or trademark use that implies sponsorship. Distinctive interiors and integrated artworks need separate review.
- For Joeville, use public architecture to learn proportions, materials, massing, and climate response; compose original facades and interiors. Do not trace a single modern building or copy its plans.

### 3.3 Whole Foods Market and other current businesses

- Whole Foods Market's official store page currently lists the **Pearl** store at **2905 Pearl St, Boulder, CO 80301**, with branded food counters and services. ([Whole Foods Market: Pearl store](https://www.wholefoodsmarket.com/stores/boulder))
- The USPTO explains that trademarks identify the source of goods or services and can include names, logos, designs, and combinations of those elements. ([USPTO: Trademark basics](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics); [USPTO: Trademark examples](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/trademark-examples))
- **Recommendation:** Do not name a Joeville set Whole Foods, copy its logo, reproduce Amazon/Prime marks, copy its branded department names, or imitate a recognizably source-identifying facade/interior package without written clearance and counsel review.
- The fictional grocery should not be a one-joke near-name or a recolored copy. Give it an independently cleared name, different exterior massing, original logo, original color system, original aisle/signage language, and a Joeville-specific story function.
- Apply the same rule to Pearl Street's real restaurants, cafés, hotels, theaters, meditation centers, and shops. Current storefronts change; if real business identity is ever intentionally depicted, conduct a fresh business-status and trademark clearance check within 30 days of picture lock.

### 3.4 Recommended clearance classification for the art bible

| Class | Treatment | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| A — geographic fact | May inform map and distant scenic grammar | Mountains west, creek corridor, canyon separation |
| B — public historical/design reference | Use as research; redraw originally | Brick pedestrian scale, Chautauqua campus pattern, historic commercial rhythm |
| C — identifiable public landmark | Legal/production review before faithful depiction | Exact Chautauqua buildings, exact Pearl nodes, exact Boulder Falls composition |
| D — branded/private property | Fictionalize by default or obtain written clearance | Whole Foods, named cafés, private interiors, branded storefronts |
| E — copyrighted art/source imagery | Do not reproduce without license | Murals, sculpture, photographer-owned images, maps used as final art |

## 4. Research gaps and required verification gates

1. **Character-scale dependency:** No environmental dimensions should be locked until the 13-character height/width, ground line, seated height, and camera/perspective references are measured.
2. **Castle Grace canon:** Its architecture, visibility, ownership, and precise map location require creator direction and inspection of any supplied Castle Grace references.
3. **Actual scout photography:** This desk research does not replace an on-site visual survey of street widths, slopes, creek banks, Chautauqua approaches, canyon transitions, and vegetation at character eye level.
4. **Boulder Falls status:** Recheck the official trail page immediately before any scout. It was listed closed on 2026-07-13.
5. **Current businesses:** Business names, tenants, hours, and storefront appearance are volatile. Recheck only if the production chooses faithful depiction; fictional analogues avoid this maintenance burden.
6. **Legal review:** Production counsel should review final landmark fidelity, fictional business names/logos, trade-dress distance, murals/public art, releases, and any promotional claim suggesting Boulder or business endorsement.
7. **Indigenous and social history:** A fuller cultural-history pass is required before the show makes narrative claims about settlement, displacement, or local identity. This dossier only establishes the scenic/history baseline needed for environment design.
